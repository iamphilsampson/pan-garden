# Handover: Phil's plant repotting and care project

**Date:** 5 August 2026
**Status:** Ten plants actioned, six outstanding. Blocked on a shopping trip.

---

## What this is

Phil's annual plant sort-out. He hadn't touched his houseplants in over a year and wanted to know which needed repotting, how many pots he already had, and how many to buy. It grew into a full audit: species identification from photos, per-plant actions, a feeding programme, soil recipes, and a shopping list.

**All detail lives in `plant-repot-plan.md`. Do not re-derive it.** There is also `plants-to-action.html`, a photo-card reference of the outstanding plants with the shopping list at the bottom, which is what he actually works from.

## The headline finding

He asked a pot-buying question. The answer turned out to be that **almost nothing needed a bigger pot.** The collection had two shared problems: not enough light, and no feed since the compost ran out over a year ago. Symptoms were long bare stems, small leaves, and growth running away from every pot.

The pot count fell at every round of evidence: 4 to 9 → 3 to 4 → 2 to 3 → 2 → **1**. Each measurement or photo removed a purchase. Hold that line: **the default answer to "should I repot this" in this collection has been no.**

## Species corrections that reframed the work

Four of his names were wrong, and each changed the care advice:

| He called it | It is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Banana palm | **Strelitzia nicolai** | Wants *less* water than a banana, and likes being pot-bound |
| Bonsai | **Pine seedling** | Needs full sun and winter cold outdoors. Indoors it dies whatever you do |
| Fridge palm | **Dracaena marginata** | Slow, likes being snug, rots if overpotted, browns from tap water |
| Blue ivy / Dino | **Pothos cultivars and a heartleaf philodendron** | Three different plants, none an ivy |

He also has **three monsteras**, not two: Prop Joe, Philipos and OG loc.

## The recurring technical thread

**Monstera aerial roots** came up repeatedly and it is the single most useful idea in the project. In air they stay thin and corky and do nothing. In moist substrate they branch into real feeder roots. In water they make brittle roots that transplant badly. All three monsteras have thick aerial roots doing nothing, so the fix everywhere is to bury them or tuck them into a damp pole. It is also why the air layer must be rooted in sphagnum, never water.

## Decisions already made, do not relitigate

- **Prop Joe:** repot into 30cm now (August); wrap the air layer **late February**; cut it free April or May. Feed before the repot, then nothing for four to six weeks after.
- **Variegated small:** measured at 16cm with only five or six leaves, so it **stays in the same pot** with fresh mix. The 15cm purchase is cancelled and the pot cascade that depended on it is dead.
- **Dracaena:** skipped entirely. It was only ever an optional 13cm and it is happier snug.
- **String of hearts:** 9cm into the 10cm white pot he owns, half-perlite mix. Coil the bare strands onto the soil and pin them rather than taking cuttings, because the strand stays fed by the parent while it roots. No trellis.
- **Two variegated pothos are not being combined.** The mature one has adult fenestrated leaves and would hold the juvenile one in juvenile form.

## Working with Phil

- He works in fast back-and-forth while physically doing the job. Answer the question, give the next step, stop. Do not pre-build later steps.
- He asks for plain language over jargon and asked for it explicitly once. "Bump" beat "node".
- **He values being challenged and being told when advice was wrong.** Several recommendations were reversed mid-project and each reversal was welcomed. Own corrections plainly rather than quietly restating.
- British English, no em dashes, no emoji.
- Photos are his main input. Ask for specific shots (whole plant, base, drainage holes) rather than generic ones. HEIC files need converting before reading; `pillow-heif` is the working route.
- He wants the plan document kept current and consistent, done items moved to the bottom.
