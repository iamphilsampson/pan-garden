The worst plant here, and the one with the most upside.

## Why 10cm and not bigger

Ceropegia are semi-succulent. They store water in their leaves and tubers and they rot in too much soil. 9cm to 10cm is barely an upsize and that is deliberate. The white pot already owned is exactly right.

## Coiling, and why it beats cuttings

Lay each long wiry strand in loose loops on top of the compost and pin every loop down so the stem is actually touching soil. At every leaf pair there is a joint, and Ceropegia root from any joint in contact with damp soil. Each rooted joint then pushes new shoots, so one bald string becomes a dozen short leafy ones coming out of the pot.

Coiling beats taking cuttings here because the strand stays attached and fed by the parent while it roots, which matters on a plant this depleted. Put everything back into rebuilding it rather than starting separate plants.

## The rest

- **Let it dry out completely between waterings.** The salt crust says the opposite problem is not the risk here
- Bare strands with widely spaced leaves is a light problem, not a water one
- **The hoop or trellis is off the shopping list.** It is already in a hanger, so once the plant is full the trailing looks after itself. The problem was never the trailing method
- Keep the live moss away from it. Moss would rot it
