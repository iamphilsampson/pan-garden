## The correction, and then the correction to that

On 21 August the plan's top-dress line was overturned in favour of a repot, on the strength of the
base photo being described as a dense root mat.

**Looking at that photo properly, it is not a mat.** It shows roughly fifteen to twenty healthy
thick roots through the drainage holes with the pot base plainly visible between them, plus two
long runners. That is roots finding the exit, which everywhere else in this collection has meant
it ran out of **fresh soil, not room**.

Three things now point the same way:

- The photo shows escaped roots, not a root-bound ball
- **The small one, from the same cutting, was pulled out on 21 August and its roots were in good
  shape and not matted.** The closest comparison available says the same
- The 21cm pot in hand is steeply tapered, so the usable volume gain over the 18cm is marginal

**So do not decide this from a photograph a third time. Take him out and look.** If the ball
resembles the small one, refresh in the same 18cm with fresh mix. If it is genuinely tight and
circling, go up a size, and find a straighter-sided pot rather than using the narrow one.

This is the collection's pattern reasserting itself. The pot count fell from nine to one because
every single time something was measured or actually looked at, the honest answer was less pot.

## Everything else stays

Adult-form leaves with marginal splits and enclosed holes. Do not combine it with the small
variegated: the mature one would hold the juvenile one in juvenile form. The metal rod it already
has is fine, so no pole and no trellis.

## The open question: pothos or Monstera

Phil is fairly confident both variegated plants are Monstera, propagated from the same cutting.
The same-cutting part is not in dispute and explains one clone in two leaf forms.

The photo evidence points to **variegated Epipremnum aureum in adult form** rather than Monstera:

- **Thin wiry petioles running straight into the blade.** No geniculum, the swollen flexible knee
  a Monstera has where petiole meets leaf, and no papery sheath
- **Thick, waxy, high-gloss leaves.** Monstera deliciosa foliage is thinner and more matte
- **Marbled variegation with yellow-green intermediate zones**, which is Marble Queen character.
  Monstera 'Albo' is crisp white against green with sharp boundaries and no yellow
- **Scale.** These leaves are around 20cm with shallow notches and the odd hole. A Monstera
  deliciosa splitting that way would be a far larger plant on thick green sheathed petioles
- Mature Epipremnum genuinely does fenestrate and lobe, and a cutting off a mature plant keeps
  making adult leaves, which fits a small plant with adult foliage exactly

**The test that settles it, and Phil has three known Monsteras in the room to compare against:**
feel for the geniculum where the petiole joins the blade. On Philipos or OG loc there is a
distinct swollen wrinkled joint that the leaf pivots on. On a pothos the petiole runs straight in
with nothing to wiggle. Check the petiole for a dried papery sheath too.

**If the geniculum is there, this is Monstera and the care changes**: chunky aroid mix instead of
the general blend, a proper pole with aerial roots tucked in rather than the metal rod, and the
collection has five monsteras rather than three.

Moderate feeder. Heavy nitrogen pushes variegated pothos greener, so do not overdo it.
