OG loc monstera, 26cm

Collapsed outward, leaves on the floor, petioles 70cm+ and weak. Jammed into a corner getting light from one side only.

Move him out of that corner. Costs nothing, takes a minute, and matters more than any stake — the corner is why every petiole bolted sideways. No pole will shorten the ones you already have; it only makes the next ones come up short and stiff. Top-dressing and pole work wait for the shop.

Soil jobs — now unblocked

You have base medium, perlite, gravel and moss, so the four top-dressings can happen today. Prop Joe is the only one still waiting on the shop: he needs the 30cm pot, more bark, and a feed before he goes in.

Prop Joe monstera, ~28cm

50 to 60cm of bare trunk, leaning 45°, dozens of aerial roots dangling in air, pole too short to touch him.

Repot into 30cm deep, glazed or terracotta. Gravel in the base for ballast. Bury 20 to 30cm of bare trunk and tuck every reachable aerial root into the compost. Bark-heavy mix, full-height pole. Feed him before the repot, then nothing for four to six weeks after. Air layer late February, cut it free April or May.

Variegated small variegated pothos, 16cm

Roots out through several drainage holes. But only five or six leaves in a 16cm pot.

Stay in the same 16cm pot. At that size the pot is already generous for the plant — roots through the holes means it has run out of fresh soil, not room. Going to 19 would leave a lot of wet compost round a small root ball, which is a rot risk on a slow variegated plant. So: ease it out, tease the outer roots loose, trim anything circling tightly, and put it back in the same pot with fresh mix. Give it its own support so it can mature like the big one.

Philipos monstera, 25cm

Sprawling unsupported. Coir pole a stub the plant passed years ago. A thick aerial root looping loose across the soil. Soil settled well below the rim.

Top-dress: scrape the top 3 to 5cm off, replace with fresh mix plus worm castings, bury that loose aerial root, top the level back to the rim. No repot, no root pruning. Taller pole via extensions.

String of hearts Ceropegia woodii, 9cm → 10cm

The worst plant in the collection. Strands almost entirely bare, several dried dead sections, white salt crust on bone-dry compacted soil.

Into the 10cm white pot, and nothing bigger — semi-succulent, rots in too much soil. Mix roughly half perlite. Cut the dead strands off. Then coil the bare strands in loose loops on top of the compost and pin each loop down so the stem touches soil: they root from every leaf joint in contact with damp soil, and each rooted joint pushes fresh shoots. One bald string becomes a dozen short leafy ones. Then the brightest spot you own, and let it dry out completely between waterings.

Shopping list

Updated after the first shop. Base medium, perlite, gravel and a crate of live moss are in, so the top-dressing jobs are unblocked. What is left below is one list, in the order worth buying it. The moss cancels every pole purchase except the pole cores — you wrap what you have rather than buying moss poles.
  1. Liquid houseplant feed. The highest-value thing on this page. Nothing has been fed in over a year and the window shuts mid-September. Any general houseplant feed will do.
  2. Slow-release granular feed. Mixed through the new compost so Prop Joe's repot and the top-dressings keep going without you remembering.
  3. 1 × 30cm pot, deep, glazed or terracotta. Prop Joe only. Weight is the point, so not plastic. Everything else keeps its pot.
  4. Orchid bark, 6L. Your 4L Westland orchid mix is part bark, part Seramis, so it does not cover it. The 30cm pot alone wants 6 to 7L of bark at 40%.
  5. Compressed coir block, if you can get one. Not a blocker any more now the Culvita is in, but worth it: bagged universal potting soil is dense and compacts, and Prop Joe's 30cm is the one pot where that hurts. Buried trunk and aerial roots need air. A 5kg block makes about 70L, so it also covers next year.
  6. Otherwise, check the litres on your two Culvita bags. Under 30L combined, grab one more. Prop Joe's 30cm takes 15 to 18L on its own, before four top-dressings. A coir block makes this moot.
  7. 1 full-height pole core for Prop Joe, plus 3 or 4 extension sections. Take one of OG loc's poles with you: white plastic sockets mean they are the extendable kind, so matching the brand fixes OG loc and Philipos too. If nothing matches, a length of 25mm plastic pipe or a stout cane works, since you are wrapping it in moss anyway.
  8. 1 long bamboo cane. Anchors OG loc's pole. It already leans at 45°, and extending a leaning pole just gives you a taller lean.
  9. Soft plant ties. Tying the monsteras in to the poles without cutting the stems.

Already in the house, do not rebuy: universal potting soil, 6L perlite, 4L orchid mix, gravel, garden twine, garden wire, worm castings, a crate of live moss, the 10cm white pot for the string of hearts.

Deliberately not buying: moss poles or coir poles (the crate covers it), sphagnum (same, and the air layer is February anyway), a 15cm pot (the small variegated is 16 and staying), a 13cm pot (the Dracaena is happier snug), more gravel (you only need 2 to 3cm in Prop Joe's base for ballast, and it is not there for drainage), horticultural grit (perlite and gravel cover it), U-pins (bent garden wire is the same thing and you have wire), a hoop or trellis (coiling the strands is the better fix), rooting hormone (roots unaided in two to three weeks), link stakes for the Strelitzia (only if the fans still splay now it has moved).

One thing that is free: put a bucket or two out for rainwater. Tap water kills sphagnum, so the surplus moss only stays alive on rainwater.
